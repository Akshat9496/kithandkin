NRI parents in Delhi NCR: a practical guide to getting support.

Kith & Kin — Elder Concierge6 min readDelhi NCR
← Back to all articles

If you are reading this from London, Toronto, Sydney, or New York, the question you are probably asking yourself is something like: "My parents are in Delhi NCR and I am not sure they are getting the support they need. What do I actually do?"

This guide is an honest, practical answer to that question.

Step 1: Understand what your parents actually need

The first mistake most NRI families make is looking for help before they have clearly defined the problem. Elder care is a broad category. Your parents may need one or more of the following — and the right kind of support depends on which:

  • Regular health monitoring — blood pressure, medication compliance, mobility
  • Companionship and social engagement
  • Practical support with tasks that have become difficult (grocery runs, bill payments, appointments)
  • Hospital and clinic accompaniment
  • Post-illness or post-surgery recovery support
  • A trusted local contact who can respond to unexpected situations
  • Regular, honest updates for the family abroad

Most families need a combination of the first, fifth, sixth, and seventh. Very few parents in Delhi NCR actually need a full-time resident nurse — though that is often the first thing families reach for out of anxiety.

Step 2: Have an honest conversation with your parents

This is the hardest step, and it is worth spending time on it.

Your parents may have strong feelings about being seen as unable to manage. Your father, who managed a team of fifty in his professional life, may resist the idea of someone visiting to "check on him." Your mother may worry about what the neighbours will think.

The framing matters enormously. You are not arranging for someone to look after them because they cannot manage — you are arranging for someone to make your life abroad less anxious. You are asking them to accept help because it is good for you, not because it is necessary for them. This framing often lands much better.

"Papa, I want to stop worrying every time I call. This is for my peace of mind as much as anything." — A real conversation a Kith & Kin client had with his father. It worked.

Step 3: Know what kinds of services exist

Delhi NCR has a growing elder care ecosystem. Understanding the categories helps you choose the right one.

Home nursing services

Provide registered nurses for post-surgical recovery, IV administration, wound care. Necessary for medical recovery; not suitable as ongoing general elder care.

Domestic helper placement

Agencies that place full-time or part-time helpers in the home. Useful for household tasks; variable quality; helpers may leave without notice.

Elder care concierge services

Structured, regular visits from trained professionals who observe, check in, handle tasks, accompany to appointments, and report back. Best suited to parents who are living independently but need a trusted point of contact — and whose family needs regular, honest updates.

Residential care facilities

Assisted living and care homes. The right option when a parent needs continuous supervision or nursing care. A significant transition that most families in Delhi NCR approach only as a last resort.

Step 4: Evaluate any service carefully

Whether you are considering Kith & Kin or any other service, ask these questions before committing:

  • Who specifically will be visiting my parents? What is their background?
  • How is each visit documented, and how quickly will I receive the report?
  • What happens if there is an emergency between visits?
  • Is the person building a genuine relationship with my parents, or completing a transaction?
  • How do you handle it if my parents are not comfortable with the visitor?
  • What is the commitment period? Is there flexibility?

Step 5: Start small

One of the most common mistakes is doing nothing because the decision feels overwhelming, and then doing too much all at once during a crisis. A panic-driven decision — made from abroad, in a hurry — rarely lands well.

The better approach: start with one or two visits per month, with the explicit purpose of finding out how your parents' day-to-day life really is. Use what you learn to decide whether more support is needed. Most families are surprised by what a first visit surfaces — and it is almost always manageable once you know.
